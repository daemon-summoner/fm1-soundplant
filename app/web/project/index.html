        What goes into the file, and what comes back

        A pattern is written at the project's tempo, 384 ticks to a quarter note, as one MIDI track after a first one that holds the tempo. A scene is one MIDI track for each track it plays (muted tracks left out), each for one pass of its pattern. A melodic track is on the MIDI channel of its number and a kit on channel 10. Each step's velocity, length and nudge are kept, and so are its up to four notes. On import the pattern's step size sets the grid; a note between steps is kept as the step's nudge, or moved onto the step if you turn that off. A groove, a step's condition and tricks, the loop window and the parameter lanes are not in the file, and a MIDI file has none of them to give.

        Using it

        Open a backup (the button, or drop the file on the box), then pick a slot. A slot that is empty on the FM-1 can't be chosen. The line under the slots says whether the backup can be saved as it is: the values are checked against what the FM-1 stores, and the flash the three projects take is counted (they share 20 sectors; a slot's patterns hold up to 22 KB packed). "Save the backup" makes a new file named after the one you opened; the original is never touched.

        On the steps, pick the track and the pattern, then a note (or a sound, on a kit track), and move over the grid with the arrow keys: Space or Enter turns the note on or off on that step. With the mouse, a click turns a step on or off, and a click on a step that is on selects it first. The panel under the grid edits the step you are on: its velocity, how long it holds, a nudge ahead of or behind the beat, and when it plays (always, a chance, or a cycle such as 2:4). A step can hold four notes.

        A track's mute belongs to the scene, as on the FM-1: the Scenes section says which pattern each track plays in a scene and which tracks that scene mutes. The song is a list of scenes, each for a number of bars; "Add a part" copies the one before it. Parts are numbered from 1 and the song plays them in order.

        In the MIDI section, pick a track and a pattern. "Save as a MIDI file" writes it (or every track of a scene) as a Standard MIDI file. "Import a MIDI file" reads one into that pattern: you see the steps it makes and what it could not hold (notes past step 64, more than four at once, drum notes with no kit sound, timing it rounded, other channels) before you press Apply, and "Undo the import" takes it back until you change something else.

        What it edits, and what it keeps

        Edited here: the tempo, the scene the project starts on, the eight tracks' names, levels and pans, the eight patterns of every track with each step's notes, velocity, length, nudge and condition, the patterns' lengths, step sizes and note lengths, the 32 scenes (in use, patterns, mutes) and the song. Not edited, and written back exactly as they were: the tracks' sounds, the conductor, the throws, the weave, the parameter lanes and the steps' tricks, the kit hits' settings, the presets, the samples, and any other parts of the file. A project's name (the old project name field) is not read by the FM-1 any more, so it is not shown; the tracks have names to edit instead.

        Only projects in the format this SoundPlant saves can be opened. If one is older, load it on the FM-1, save it there, and make the backup again.

        Putting it back

        Restore the saved file with the Backup page: it writes the projects back after the FM-1 says yes on its screen, and replaces what is in them. Keep the original backup until you have heard the edited project on your FM-1.

Samples

Your own samples on the FM-1, up to 32 of them. On a track they come after SoundPlant's own: turn the sample engine's sample knob (or a drum key's, on the sample model) past the factory ones to user 1, user 2 and so on.

Connect the FM-1 by USB with SoundPlant running, and use Chrome or Edge. WAV, AIFF, MP3 and FLAC files work.

The breaks

Since SoundPlant 0.7 its four breaks are no longer built into the firmware, to make room for new sounds. "Add the breaks" puts them in your bank, then "Send to the FM-1". The slice engine's presets and your projects find them there by name, whatever their place in the list. Without them, those presets are silent and the screen says "install the breaks". The breaks are free loops posted by u/FCTRYBEATS, with permission to use them in any project.

Preparing a sample

"Prepare a sample" opens a file in a small editor, and "Edit" on a sample in the list opens that one. Trim it (drag the two handles, use the arrow keys on one, or type the sample numbers; with "Snap cuts to zero crossings" a cut lands where the sound crosses zero, so it doesn't click), set its level (Normalise to a peak, and a gain), fade its ends, reverse it, mix a stereo file down to mono or take one side, set its loop, and play the result before it goes in. "Add to the bank" or "Update" puts it in your list; nothing reaches the FM-1 until Send, and one that is too big for the free room is refused there, with the size. Without the FM-1 connected you can still prepare a file and download it as a WAV (its loop goes in the file).

The bank keeps one loop for each sample, a start and an end, which the sample engine's loop mode plays, and a bar count for the slice engine. It has no slice markers: the slice engine cuts a loop into equal slices as it plays, so trim a loop to its exact length. A sample already in the bank is edited as it is stored there, at its rate and with its tone, and the edit does not change them again. The preview plays the sound as you have prepared it; the bank stores it at a lower rate, so on the FM-1 it is a little duller.

How samples play

Root is the note that plays a sample at its own pitch on a synth track (a note in the file's name sets it: "piano_C4.wav"). Loop says the file is a loop of that many bars, for the slice engine: it then plays at the song's tempo, slice 1 on the lowest key. A loop stored in a WAV file is kept for the sample engine's loop mode.

Sending replaces the whole bank: the samples already there are kept exactly as they are. The FM-1's sound stops for a moment while it writes. Removing or moving a sample renumbers the ones after it, so a track that used one plays its new neighbour instead. The bank holds about 15 seconds at 22 kHz, 29 at 11 kHz.
